# Questions I can answer about the project

These are questions I invite an admissions reader or technical reviewer to ask me without
advance preparation. They test ownership of the reasoning rather than typing speed.

1. Why can a source-age filter not repair an event admitted before receive time?
2. What exact inequality must a causal as of join satisfy?
3. Why are 96,494 test rows not 96,494 independent observations?
4. Why do five random seeds measure optimization sensitivity rather than replication?
5. Why is reporting the lowest of seven test calibration scores a selection problem?
6. Why is the market forecast the correct paired baseline for the TCN residual?
7. Why did the combined two-slot winner have to be added to the 846-candidate matrix?
8. What null hypothesis and maximum statistic does White's Reality Check use here?
9. Why use a stationary day bootstrap, and what does mean block length three assume?
10. Why is 32 days still a weak basis for a trading conclusion?
11. Why must an adverse-fill stress keep the original decisions fixed?
12. How do missing and 62-second-old books bias an execution audit optimistically?
13. What do the July shadow logs demonstrate, and what do they not demonstrate?
14. Which result would change my conclusion, and what must be locked before collecting it?
15. What are the three strongest limitations of the current evidence, and how would I
    address them in the next experiment?

My concise answers are not published here because memorized prose would be weak ownership
evidence. The point is that I can derive and defend the answers from the research design,
code, and artifacts in a live conversation.
